What's new here

Six things a signing app doesn't do.

Most e-signature tools capture a signature on a file. bSignX treats the agreement, the negotiation and the payment as one signed record.

Coming

Sign and settle in one go

The payment is an atomic swap tied to the agreed contract's hash. Both sides get what was agreed in one transaction, or neither does. No escrow agent. No "invoice later". A small capped swap is in testing today.

Coming

Negotiation you can verify

Every counteroffer is a new version, signed by its author and linked to the one before. Signatures never carry over. You can see exactly who agreed to what, and when.

Coming

Encrypted end to end

Each version is encrypted to the other person's own wallet key. Our server only passes sealed envelopes along. It can't read them or change them.

Coming

Private text, public proof

When you both sign, the agreed version can be checked by anyone: an auditor, a court, a register. The words stay encrypted to the two of you.

Live

b drafts. You sign.

b, our AI agent, writes the contract from what you tell it. b can redraft as often as you like. Only people sign or pay. Voice requests are coming.

Live

Sealed on chain

A signed contract is sealed on Bitcoin, encrypted to the parties. It sits in both wallets, not in our database.

Three systems, or one record.

A plain comparison of the usual steps. Each tool on the left does its own job well. bSignX joins the steps.

  • Usually

    Email drafts back and forth, then an e-signature envelope, then a bank transfer, sometimes an escrow agent. The record of what was agreed and the record of payment live in different places.

  • bSignX

How it works

Five steps, one agreed version.

  1. Tell b what you want

    Who it's for and what you're agreeing. Type it in a bChatX room, or hold to talk.

    Live

  2. b drafts the contract

    A written contract you can read in full before anything happens. If it doesn't say what you meant, ask for changes.

    Live

  3. Sign and send

    You sign in your wallet, with a drawn signature on phone or in the extension. The other person gets it to countersign.

    Live

  4. Counteroffer, or agree

    They can sign, refuse, or change the terms. A change makes a new version, signed only by them, marked COUNTEROFFER in large letters. Older versions can't be signed.

    Coming

  5. Both sign, seal, settle

    When you've both signed the same version, it's sealed on chain, encrypted to you both, and the swap pays against that exact version.

    Coming

Fees: the network fee is shown before you sign. Nothing is paid until you approve it in your wallet.

No one in the middle

Nobody can swap the paper under your pen.

Not us, not anyone between you and the other person.

  • Your wallet reads the hash itself. It works out the fingerprint from the exact contract you see. It never takes our word for it.
  • Every version is signed by its author. If the check fails, you see "NOT VERIFIED, don't sign" and there is no Sign button.
  • Keys are pinned. Your wallet remembers the other person's key. If it ever changes, you get a loud warning.
  • A chain, not a pile. Each version names the one before. Nothing can be slipped in, replayed or rolled back.
  • The payment checks the contract. Your wallet refuses a swap that doesn't match the version you signed.
  • Our server is a mailbox. It holds no key that can sign, read or pay.

One trade-off, stated plainly: when b drafts for you, b sees that text. If you'd rather it didn't, write the contract yourself.
